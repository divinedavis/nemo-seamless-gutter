Gutter Falling Off Your House: Who to Call in York County, PA

A gutter pulling away from the fascia is usually a fastener or wood-rot problem, and the fix runs anywhere from a short service call to a full replacement depending on what the wood underneath looks like.

Who you actually call, and what to do in the meantime

For a gutter that has sagged, pulled loose at one end, or is hanging by a corner, the right call is a seamless gutter contractor. That is the trade that owns the hangers, the fascia connection, the pitch, and the downspout tie-in. A roofer is the right call only if the problem started with the roof edge, drip edge, or shingles. A general handyman can screw a section back up, but if the fascia board behind it is soft, the screws will pull out again within a season.

Call NEMO Seamless Gutter at (717) 578-0073 and describe what you are seeing: which side of the house, how long the loose run is, whether it is one story or two, and whether water is now dumping against the foundation or into a doorway. That is usually enough to tell you over the phone whether this is a quick re-hang or something bigger.

In the meantime, do not go up a ladder to grab a hanging gutter. A wet, debris-filled section is heavier than it looks, and pulling on it while balanced on a ladder is how people get hurt. If a piece is hanging over a walkway or a driveway where a car parks, move the car and keep people away from underneath it. If it is safe to reach from the ground, clear anything ponding in the section so the weight comes down.

  • Seamless gutter contractor: sagging, pulled-away, separated seams, loose downspouts, bad pitch, fascia damage
  • Roofer: shingles lifting at the eave, drip edge missing, leak showing up inside near the roofline
  • Both, sometimes: an ice-dam winter that damaged the roof edge and tore the gutter off with it
  • Your insurance carrier: only if a storm, a falling limb, or a vehicle caused it in a single identifiable event

Why gutters pull away from the house

There are only a handful of real causes, and which one you have is what determines the price. Older homes around York County very often have spike-and-ferrule gutters, where a long nail is driven through the gutter into the fascia. Aluminum expands and contracts with our temperature swings, the spikes work their way loose over years, and eventually one lets go and the rest follow. That is the single most common reason a gutter starts hanging.

The second common cause is weight. A clogged gutter full of wet leaves, shingle grit, and standing water gets heavy fast, and a January freeze turns that into a solid block of ice. Hangers spaced too far apart, which is typical on older installs, cannot carry that load. The third cause is rot. Water that overflows the back of the gutter runs down the fascia board instead of away from it, and after a few years the wood is soft enough that no fastener will hold.

  • Spikes and ferrules backing out of the fascia over time
  • Hangers spaced too far apart for the load, commonly 36 inches or more on older work
  • Rotted or spongy fascia board with nothing solid to screw into
  • Ice and debris weight, especially on north-facing runs and under heavy tree cover
  • Ladder damage, where a ladder was rested directly on the gutter lip
  • Storm damage from a limb, high wind, or sliding snow off a metal roof

What it costs to fix in York County, 2026 ranges

These are honest ranges for south-central Pennsylvania, not a quote. The number for your house depends on how many feet are involved, one story or two, roof pitch and access, whether the fascia is sound, and what material is already up there. A gutter that just needs re-hanging is a very different job from one where the wood behind it has to be rebuilt first.

The most important variable is the fascia. Two houses with an identical hanging gutter can be hundreds of dollars apart because one has solid wood and the other has ten feet of rot that has to come off and be replaced before anything can be fastened back up. That is not something anyone can tell you accurately over the phone, which is why the estimate is on-site and why it is free.

  • Service call to re-secure a loose run with hidden hangers: roughly $175 to $400 for a short section on a one-story elevation
  • Re-hanging and re-pitching a longer run, 25 to 50 feet: roughly $350 to $800 depending on height and access
  • Fascia board repair or replacement: roughly $12 to $28 per linear foot for the carpentry, more if soffit is involved
  • Aluminum fascia and soffit wrap after repair: roughly $8 to $16 per linear foot
  • Replacing a damaged run with new 5-inch seamless K-style: roughly $8 to $14 per linear foot installed
  • 6-inch seamless with oversized downspouts: roughly $10 to $18 per linear foot installed
  • Downspout replacement or rerouting: roughly $8 to $15 per linear foot, plus extensions and underground tie-ins
  • Whole-house replacement on a typical 150 to 200 foot home: roughly $1,500 to $3,600 in 5-inch, higher in 6-inch or with heavy fascia work
  • Two-story, steep-pitch, or difficult ladder access commonly adds 15 to 30 percent

Repair the section or replace the run

If one end of a gutter has dropped and everything else is straight, tight, and draining, a repair is usually the right answer. New hidden hangers screwed into sound wood at proper spacing will outlast the original spike install by a wide margin, and it is a fraction of the cost of replacement.

Replacement starts making more sense when the aluminum itself is compromised. If a gutter has been hanging for a while, it is often bent, kinked, or twisted along the top edge, and a bent gutter will never hold pitch correctly no matter how well you fasten it. Sectional gutter with multiple leaking seams, corrosion at the corners, or undersized 4-inch gutter on a large roof are all situations where putting money into a repair is money spent twice.

There is also a middle path people forget about. You do not have to replace every gutter on the house at once. Replacing the failed elevation in seamless aluminum and leaving the sound sides alone is a legitimate option, and a good contractor will tell you when that is the smarter spend.

  • Lean toward repair: one loose run, straight aluminum, solid fascia, gutter is properly sized
  • Lean toward replacement: bent or kinked sections, repeated seam leaks, corrosion, undersized gutter, or a run that has come loose more than once
  • Address the fascia first either way, because new gutter fastened to rotted wood is a temporary fix at full price
  • Ask about hanger spacing on any new work, since tighter spacing is what keeps this from happening again

Common questions

Is a gutter falling off an emergency?

It is urgent rather than an emergency in most cases. The safety issue is anything hanging over a walkway, door, or parked car. The property issue is that a detached gutter dumps concentrated water at the foundation, which over a wet stretch can mean a damp basement, eroded landscaping, or settling. If the section is not hanging over anywhere people walk, it can usually wait a few days for a proper scheduled repair rather than a rushed one.
