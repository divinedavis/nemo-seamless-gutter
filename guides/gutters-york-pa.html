What gutter work costs in York County in 2026

Gutters are priced by the linear foot, then adjusted for height, access, material and how much of the old system has to come off. The ranges below are what homeowners in and around York are generally seeing in 2026. They are ranges on purpose: two houses on the same street can be 40 percent apart once you factor in a second story, a steep roof pitch, or rotted fascia hiding behind the old gutter.

A common York County job — a single-story ranch or a modest two-story colonial with 150 to 220 linear feet of gutter and four to six downspouts — usually lands somewhere between $1,800 and $5,000 for a full seamless replacement. Add guards and the top of that range moves up. Copper, half-round, or a three-story wrap moves it up considerably.

  • 5-inch seamless aluminum K-style, installed: roughly $9 to $15 per linear foot
  • 6-inch seamless aluminum K-style, installed: roughly $12 to $19 per linear foot
  • Half-round aluminum: roughly $18 to $32 per linear foot
  • Copper (seamless or half-round): roughly $35 to $65+ per linear foot
  • 2x3 downspouts: roughly $10 to $18 per foot of drop; 3x4 oversized: roughly $13 to $24
  • Tear-off and haul-away of existing gutter: roughly $1 to $2.50 per linear foot
  • Fascia board replacement where wood has rotted: roughly $9 to $22 per linear foot
  • Gutter guards: roughly $8 to $25 per linear foot depending on the product
  • Repairs and resealing on an otherwise sound system: often a few hundred dollars rather than a replacement

What actually moves your number up or down

If you want to predict where in those ranges your house falls, these are the variables that matter most. None of them are upsells — they are the physical reality of the building.

Height and access are usually the biggest swing. Work that can be done off ladders on a single story is fast. A three-story gable over a deck, a bank-barn style rear elevation, or landscaping that keeps ladders away from the wall all add labor and sometimes staging.

  • Linear footage and number of corners — miters are slower and more failure-prone than straight runs, so a house with many roof planes costs more per foot
  • Stories and roof pitch, plus whatever is underneath the eave
  • Gutter size: 6-inch with 3x4 downspouts costs more per foot but moves roughly 40 percent more water, which matters under large or steep roof areas
  • Condition of the fascia and roof edge — we cannot hang new gutter on soft wood, and that is only visible once the old gutter comes off
  • Drainage at the bottom: splash blocks, buried extensions, tie-ins to existing underground drains, or getting water away from a basement wall
  • Material and finish — standard .027 aluminum versus heavier .032, standard colors versus a special-order match
  • Whether guards are added, and which type
  • How much of the existing system is being reused; partial replacements price differently than a full wrap

5-inch or 6-inch, and why seamless matters here

Seamless means the gutter is formed on site from a coil, in one continuous piece per run, so the only joints in your system are at corners and end caps. Sectional gutter from a big-box store has a seam every ten feet, and seams are where leaks start. That is the single biggest durability difference between the two approaches, and it is why almost every professional install in York County is seamless.

On sizing: 5-inch K-style handles most York County roofs fine. Six-inch is worth the upcharge when you have a large or steep roof plane draining into one run, when you have had overflow at valleys during heavy summer storms, or when you are adding fine-mesh guards, which slightly reduce intake. If you have watched a gutter shoot water over the front edge during a downpour, the problem is often capacity or downspout count rather than the gutter itself, and going to 6-inch with 3x4 downspouts is usually the fix.

Color is a smaller decision than most people expect but it lasts twenty-plus years. We will bring color options to the estimate so you can hold them against your trim and siding in daylight rather than choosing off a screen.

Gutter guards: when they are worth it

York County has a lot of mature oak, maple and tulip poplar, and the difference between a house with trees over the roof and one without is the difference between cleaning twice a year and cleaning almost never. Guards are worth pricing if you are already replacing gutter, because installing them at the same time avoids paying twice for access.

Be skeptical of any guard sold as fully maintenance-free. Fine micro-mesh handles shingle grit, seed pods and needles well but still needs an occasional rinse. Simple screens are cheap and keep out leaves but let small debris through. Reverse-curve and foam products each have tradeoffs. We would rather explain those tradeoffs at your house than sell you the most expensive option by default.

  • Basic screens or perforated aluminum: lower cost, keeps out whole leaves, still passes grit and seeds
  • Stainless micro-mesh: the most reliable option under heavy tree cover, higher per-foot cost
  • Retrofit onto sound existing gutter is possible and cheaper than a full replacement
  • If your gutters already pitch wrong or leak at the seams, guards will not fix that and should not be installed over it

Common questions

How much does it cost to replace all the gutters on a house in York, PA?

Most full seamless replacements in York County land between roughly $1,800 and $5,000, based on about $9 to $15 per linear foot for 5-inch aluminum or $12 to $19 for 6-inch, plus downspouts and tear-off. A large two-story with many corners, oversized downspouts and guards can exceed that. A small ranch with straightforward access can come in under it. A measured on-site estimate is the only way to know which end of that range applies to your house.

Do I need a full replacement, or can my gutters be repaired?

If the gutter body is sound and the problem is a leaking corner, a loose hanger, wrong pitch, or a detached downspout, repair is usually the better value and often costs a few hundred dollars. Replacement makes more sense when the gutter is sectional and leaking at multiple seams, when the metal is dented or split along a run, or when the fascia behind it has failed. We will tell you which situation you are in even when repair is the smaller job.

How long does a gutter installation take?

Most single-family replacements in York County are a one-day job. Larger homes, multiple elevations with difficult access, or jobs that turn up fascia repair can run into a second day. Because the gutter is formed on site, there is no waiting on a manufacturing lead time for standard aluminum; special-order colors and copper do take longer to source.
