How Much Does LeafFilter Cost Per Foot in York County, PA?

LeafFilter does not publish pricing, but installed quotes are commonly reported in the $17 to $45 per linear foot range, and the spread depends far more on your house than on the product.

The short answer on per-foot pricing

LeafFilter is sold through in-home sales appointments and the company does not publish a rate card, so there is no official per-foot figure to quote. Based on what homeowners across the mid-Atlantic report, installed pricing generally falls somewhere between roughly $17 and $45 per linear foot, with a lot of quotes landing in the $20 to $35 range after whatever discount is applied at the appointment.

That is the price of the guard installed on gutters that are in usable condition. If the gutters need to be replaced first, or if the roof edge needs work, that is a separate line item and the total per-foot cost of the project goes up accordingly.

We install our own seamless gutters and micro-mesh guards, so treat the numbers below as an honest attempt to explain the market rather than a competitor's sales pitch.

  • Commonly reported LeafFilter installed range: about $17 to $45 per linear foot
  • Most quotes cluster around $20 to $35 per foot after in-home discounting
  • Minimum job charges are common, so short runs cost more per foot
  • Gutter replacement, fascia repair, and roof-edge work are priced separately

Why the per-foot number swings so widely

A per-foot price is an average, and averages hide the things that actually drive labor. Two houses on the same street in Dallastown can be a thousand dollars apart on identical footage because one is a walkable single story and the other has three stories at the back with a steep roof over a deck.

When you are comparing quotes, ask each contractor which of these apply to your house and how they affected the price. A company that can explain the number is usually a company that measured.

  • Total linear footage, and whether the job is above or below any minimum charge
  • Height and access: single story versus two, three, or a walkout basement elevation
  • Roof pitch and whether ladders can be set safely, or staging is required
  • Gutter size and profile: 5-inch K-style, 6-inch, or half-round
  • Condition of the existing gutters and whether they can carry a rigid guard at all
  • Inside and outside corners, dormers, valleys, and short offset runs, which slow the work down
  • Whether existing screens, foam inserts, or old helmet-style covers have to be removed and hauled off
  • Fascia rot or loose spikes discovered once the old guard or gutter comes off
  • Financing: a monthly payment plan can add meaningfully to what you pay over the term

What that looks like on a real York County house

Most single-family homes in York County carry somewhere between 120 and 220 linear feet of gutter. A modest ranch in Red Lion might be 130 feet. A two-story colonial with a garage wing and several dormers in Springettsbury or Jacobus can easily hit 200 feet or more.

Run the arithmetic before the appointment so the total does not surprise you. At $25 per foot, 160 feet of gutter guard is about $4,000. At $35 per foot, the same house is about $5,600. Those are real, normal numbers for a full-perimeter micro-mesh installation from a national brand, and they are the reason it is worth understanding what you are paying for.

  • 130 feet at $20 to $35 per foot: roughly $2,600 to $4,550
  • 160 feet at $20 to $35 per foot: roughly $3,200 to $5,600
  • 200 feet at $20 to $35 per foot: roughly $4,000 to $7,000
  • Add gutter replacement if the existing gutters are undersized, sagging, or rusted through

What local micro-mesh and other guard styles cost instead

Micro-mesh is a category, not a single brand. Stainless steel mesh over a rigid frame is the design that actually keeps out shingle grit, maple seeds, and the pine needles that are the real problem on wooded lots in southern York County. Several manufacturers build guards to that standard, and a local contractor installing them typically prices well below a national in-home sales operation because there is no commission structure or media budget in the number.

Installed by a local company in this market, quality micro-mesh generally runs in the $8 to $18 per linear foot range depending on the product, the height, and the condition of the gutters underneath. Cheaper guard styles exist and cost less, but they solve a narrower problem.

New seamless aluminum gutter is its own line item. In York County, 5-inch K-style typically runs about $8 to $14 per foot installed and 6-inch runs about $11 to $18, with downspouts, height, tear-off, and fascia repair moving those figures.

  • Stainless micro-mesh, professionally installed: roughly $8 to $18 per linear foot
  • Aluminum or plastic screen: roughly $3 to $7 per linear foot
  • Foam or brush inserts: roughly $2 to $6 per linear foot, and prone to clogging with fine debris
  • Reverse-curve or helmet-style covers: often priced similarly to micro-mesh, with more overflow risk in heavy rain
  • 5-inch seamless gutter installed: roughly $8 to $14 per linear foot
  • 6-inch seamless gutter installed: roughly $11 to $18 per linear foot

Whoever you get quotes from, ask for the total price, the price per linear foot, the measured footage, the guard manufacturer and mesh material, and the warranty terms in writing. A quote that includes all five is a quote you can compare.

  • Ask what the guard is made of: stainless mesh, aluminum mesh, plastic, or foam

Common questions

Why won't LeafFilter give me a price over the phone?

Their model is built around an in-home appointment where a representative measures the house and presents a price on the spot. That is a legitimate way to sell a measured product, since footage and access genuinely change the cost. The practical effect is that you cannot comparison shop without sitting through the appointment. If you want a number before committing to a visit, use the $17 to $45 per foot range against your own rough footage to set expectations.

Is a national brand's micro-mesh guard better than what a local contractor installs?

The design matters more than the logo. What you want is a stainless steel micro-mesh surface on a rigid frame, fastened in a way that does not depend on lifting your shingles. Several manufacturers make guards to that standard and local contractors install them. Compare mesh material, frame rigidity, how the guard attaches, and the warranty rather than the brand name.
