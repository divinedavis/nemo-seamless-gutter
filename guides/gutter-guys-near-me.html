What gutter work actually costs around York County in 2026

Gutters are priced by the foot, not by the house, which is why nobody can quote you over the phone without knowing your footage, your roofline, and how many stories they are working from. That said, you should not have to call five companies just to find out whether you are looking at a $2,000 job or a $6,000 job. Below are the ranges we see hold up across York, Hanover, Red Lion, Dover, Shrewsbury, and the rest of the county.

Every one of these is a range because the factors underneath them move. Treat them as a way to sanity-check the quotes you collect, not as a bid.

  • 5-inch seamless aluminum, installed: roughly $9 to $16 per linear foot
  • 6-inch oversized seamless aluminum, installed: roughly $12 to $20 per linear foot
  • 2x3 downspouts: roughly $9 to $15 per foot; 3x4 downspouts: roughly $12 to $19 per foot
  • Whole-house replacement, typical York County home with 140 to 200 feet of gutter: commonly $2,000 to $4,500
  • Larger, steeper, or three-story homes with complex rooflines: $4,500 to $8,000 and up
  • Copper or specialty half-round systems: $30 to $60+ per foot, a different category of project entirely
  • Tear-off and haul-away of the old system: often built into the per-foot price, sometimes billed at $1 to $2 per foot

How to tell the real gutter contractors from the truck-and-ladder crowd

Gutter work has a low barrier to entry. Anyone can rent a machine or buy sectional gutter at a big-box store and call themselves a gutter guy. The difference shows up two winters later, when the seams leak, the hangers pull, and the fascia behind the gutter has gone soft.

You do not need to be an expert to screen for quality. You just need to ask a few specific questions and listen to whether the answers are concrete.

  • Do they run seamless gutter on-site from a coil, or are they installing sectional lengths joined every ten feet? Seams are where gutters fail first.
  • What gauge aluminum? Ask for .027 or heavier. Thin .025 coil dents from a ladder lean.
  • How are they hanging it? Hidden hangers screwed into the rafter tails or solid fascia, spaced roughly every 24 inches, is the standard. Spike-and-ferrule is dated.
  • Will they look at the fascia and drip edge before quoting, or just measure the run? Hanging new gutter on rotted wood is the most common shortcut in this trade.
  • Are they carrying liability insurance, and will they show you the certificate without a fuss?
  • Is the quote itemized by footage, downspout count, and materials, or is it one number on the back of a card?
  • Are they local enough to come back? A leaking miter in March is a five-minute fix for someone based in York County and a scheduling problem for someone driving in from two counties over.

What moves your number up or down

When two quotes on the same house come in $1,500 apart, it is almost always one of the items below, not a difference in greed. Knowing which ones apply to your house lets you read your estimates intelligently.

Access is the single biggest swing factor most homeowners do not anticipate. A walkable one-story ranch in Dover and a steep three-story in the older parts of York City can have identical footage and very different labor.

  • Total linear footage and number of downspouts, which is the base of the whole price
  • Height and roof pitch, which determine whether the crew works from ladders, from the roof, or needs staging
  • Corners and miters. A simple rectangle prices lower per foot than a house with dormers, bays, and multiple roof planes
  • Fascia condition. Soft or rotted board has to be replaced before anything gets hung, commonly $8 to $20 per foot for the affected sections
  • 5-inch versus 6-inch. Six-inch gutter with 3x4 downspouts handles roughly 40 percent more water and is worth considering under large or steep roof areas
  • Landscaping, decks, and drainage. Underground extensions, splash blocks, and rerouting water away from a foundation are separate line items
  • Color and material. Standard aluminum colors price alike; custom colors and heavier gauge cost more
  • Season. Late fall is the busiest stretch in south-central PA, and lead times stretch accordingly

Guards, repairs, and the smaller jobs

Not everyone searching for gutter guys needs a full replacement. A lot of York County homes need one bad run replaced, a few miters resealed, or a decision made about guards under the oaks and maples that are everywhere in this part of the state.

On guards specifically: the honest answer is that they reduce cleaning frequency rather than eliminate it, and cheap plastic screen inserts tend to cause more problems than they solve. Quality micro-mesh over a properly pitched gutter is a real improvement. Any product marketed as never needing maintenance is overselling.

  • Professional-grade gutter guards, installed: roughly $8 to $25 per foot depending on the product
  • Guards added at the same time as new gutter usually price better than a separate trip later
  • Section replacement or re-hanging a sagging run: commonly $200 to $700 depending on length and access
  • Reseal and repair calls: most contractors carry a minimum, often $150 to $300, because the truck and ladder cost the same either way
  • Gutter cleaning: roughly $150 to $350 for a typical home, more for three stories or heavy debris
  • Downspout extensions and underground tie-ins: quoted per job based on run length and what is buried out there
