How Much Do Seamless Gutters Cost in York County, PA

Most seamless gutter jobs in York County run in a wide range because the price depends on linear footage, material, and how the house is built, so here is what actually moves the number.

Typical Price Range for the Area

For a standard one or two-story house in York County with average trim work, aluminum seamless gutters usually land between about $7 and $14 per linear foot installed in 2026, with downspouts included in that range. A full gutter job on a typical single-family home, roughly 150 to 200 linear feet, often comes out to somewhere between $1,500 and $4,000 total.

That is a starting range, not a quote. Roofline complexity, house height, and material choice can push a job above or below it, which is exactly why an on-site look matters more than a number pulled off the internet.

What Pushes the Price Up or Down

The per-foot number you see quoted online rarely accounts for the things that actually change labor and material cost on a specific house. In York County, the most common factors are:

  • Linear footage of gutter needed, since it is the main driver of total cost
  • Number of stories and how steep or steep-pitched the roof is, which affects access and labor time
  • Condition of the existing fascia board, since rotten or damaged wood needs repair or replacement before gutters go up
  • Number and length of downspouts required to move water away from the foundation
  • Ease of access around the house, including landscaping, fencing, or tight side yards
  • Whether old gutters need to be removed and hauled away

Cost by Material

Material is the other big lever on price. Aluminum is by far the most common choice for homes in this region because it holds up well in Pennsylvania winters and is available in a wide range of colors, but steel and copper are options for specific situations.

  • 5-inch aluminum: generally the lowest cost option, well suited to smaller roofs with moderate water flow
  • 6-inch aluminum: handles more water volume, common on larger roofs or homes with steep rooflines, and typically runs somewhat higher per foot than 5-inch
  • Steel (galvanized or galvalume): more resistant to dents and impact, priced above standard aluminum
  • Copper: highest cost by a wide margin, chosen mainly for historic homes or specific aesthetic goals, often several times the cost of aluminum per foot

Add-Ons That Change the Total

A few common extras get bundled into gutter jobs and each adds to the total in its own way.

  • Gutter guards or leaf protection, priced separately per linear foot and optional on most jobs
  • Fascia or soffit repair, priced by the section replaced when wood is soft or damaged
  • Extending or rerouting downspouts to carry water further from the foundation
  • Color matching or custom trim work to match existing house colors

Common questions

What is the average cost to gutter a whole house in York County

For a typical single-family home, total cost for a full seamless gutter job commonly falls between $1,500 and $4,000, depending on linear footage, material, and the condition of the fascia. Larger homes, steep rooflines, or copper material can push well above that range.

How much more do 6-inch gutters cost compared to 5-inch

6-inch aluminum gutters generally cost more per linear foot than 5-inch because they use more material and often require larger downspouts. The difference varies by supplier and job, which is another reason a measured estimate is more useful than a flat rule.

Does gutter guard installation add much to the price

Gutter guards are typically priced per linear foot as a separate line item from the gutters themselves. Whether they make sense depends on the tree cover around the house and how much debris the gutters typically collect.

Why do online price calculators not match what I get quoted locally

Most national cost calculators use averaged data from many regions and do not account for York County labor rates, local material costs, or the specific condition of a given roofline and fascia. A local on-site estimate accounts for all of that directly.

Is it worth replacing fascia at the same time as the gutters

If the fascia is soft, rotted, or pulling away from the house, replacing it during the same job usually costs less than doing it as a separate project later, since the crew is already set up for the work. An estimator can tell you during the on-site visit whether your fascia needs attention.
