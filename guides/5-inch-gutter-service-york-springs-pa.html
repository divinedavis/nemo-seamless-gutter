What 5 inch gutter work typically costs here

NEMO Seamless Gutter is based in York County and works York Springs and the surrounding Adams and northern York County area. The numbers below are honest 2026 ranges for this part of south-central Pennsylvania. They are not a quote for your house, because nobody can price gutter accurately from a keyboard. They exist so you can tell a fair bid from a padded one.

Most whole-house 5 inch seamless aluminum replacements on a single-story or story-and-a-half home in this area land somewhere in the low-to-mid four figures once downspouts, corners, and haul-away are included. A larger two-story with steep roof planes and a lot of inside corners can run well past that.

  • 5 inch K-style seamless aluminum, standard .027 gauge, installed: roughly $9 to $15 per linear foot
  • Heavier .032 gauge aluminum: add roughly $2 to $4 per foot
  • 2x3 or 3x4 downspouts: roughly $10 to $16 per foot, or about $90 to $175 per typical run
  • Small repair visits (resealing seams, re-pitching a run, replacing hangers, reattaching a downspout): commonly $175 to $500
  • Gutter cleaning on a typical home: commonly $150 to $375 depending on height, roof pitch, and debris load
  • Gutter guards on 5 inch: roughly $8 to $25 per foot installed, depending heavily on the product
  • Rotted fascia board replacement found during tear-off: often $12 to $28 per foot of affected board

What actually moves your number up or down

Two houses with the same footprint can be a thousand dollars apart. These are the factors that do it, and any estimator worth hiring will walk the house and look at all of them before quoting.

  • Total linear feet, and how many corners are in it. Miters cost more than straight runs, and a cut-up roofline with dormers has a lot of them.
  • Height and access. Second and third story runs, steep pitches, decks, sunrooms, and landscaping that keeps ladders off the wall all add labor and sometimes staging.
  • Number and length of downspouts. Long ground-level runs, kick-outs, and tie-ins to underground drains add material and time.
  • Gauge and finish. Standard aluminum, heavier .032 aluminum, and copper are three very different price tiers.
  • Condition of the fascia and roof edge. Soft or rotted wood behind the old gutter has to be dealt with before new gutter goes up, and that is not visible until the old gutter comes down.
  • Tear-off and disposal of the existing gutter, which is usually included but should be stated on the estimate either way.
  • Guards, if you want them. On a wooded lot near the Conewago or up against tree lines, guards are often the difference between a system you never touch and one you clean twice a year.

5 inch versus 6 inch: when the upgrade is worth it

Five inch K-style handles the roof area on the large majority of homes in this region and is the right call most of the time. Six inch costs more per foot, uses larger 3x4 downspouts, and is worth discussing in specific situations rather than as a blanket upgrade.

The honest test is roof area draining to each run, roof pitch, and how much overflow you are actually seeing. If your existing 5 inch gutter only overflows in one spot during heavy rain, the fix is often an added downspout or corrected pitch, not a whole new size.

  • Consider 6 inch when: large uninterrupted roof planes drain into a single run, the roof is steep enough to throw water past a 5 inch lip, or valleys dump concentrated flow into one section
  • Stay with 5 inch when: the roofline is broken up, downspout placement is adequate, and the current problem is pitch, clogs, or too few outlets
  • Expect roughly a 15 to 30 percent per-foot increase moving from 5 inch to 6 inch, plus the larger downspouts

Repair, clean, or replace

Replacing gutter that has plenty of life left is a waste of your money, and patching gutter that is done costs you twice. A quick way to sort it out before anyone shows up:

Seamless gutter has no seams along the run, so leaks generally show up at corners, end caps, and outlets. Those are sealable. Sagging between hangers is usually a hanger or pitch problem. Water running behind the gutter and down the fascia usually points to a drip edge or roof edge detail, not the gutter itself.

  • Repair territory: isolated leaks at miters or end caps, a few loose hangers, a detached downspout, one section pitched wrong
  • Cleaning territory: water overflowing in heavy rain with the gutter otherwise straight and sound, especially on wooded lots
  • Replacement territory: widespread separation from the fascia, corroded or split channel, gutter that has pulled wood off with it, or old sectional gutter leaking at every joint
  • Address it either way before winter. Ice sitting in a clogged 5 inch gutter through a York County freeze-thaw cycle is what turns a cleaning into a replacement.

Can you give me a price over the phone?

We can give you the ranges on this page and tell you which end of them your house is likely to land in based on stories, roof style, and rough footage. A firm number requires measuring the actual runs and looking at the fascia, which is what the free on-site estimate is for.

How long does a 5 inch seamless gutter replacement take?

Most single-family homes in this area are a one-day job, including tear-off of the old gutter and haul-away. Larger homes, extensive fascia repair, or guard installation can push it into a second day.

Are gutter guards worth the added cost?

It depends on your trees. On an open lot with little overhang, standard cleaning is usually the cheaper long-term choice. On a wooded property, or on a two or three story section where cleaning means renting or hiring out ladder work every year, guards often pay for themselves. Guards reduce cleaning, they do not eliminate all maintenance, and anyone claiming otherwise is overselling.

What happens if you find rotted fascia after the old gutter comes off?

We stop, show you what is there, and price the repair before continuing. Fascia condition is genuinely not knowable until the old gutter is down, so a good estimate flags it as a possibility rather than pretending it cannot happen. Repairs commonly run in the range of $12 to $28 per foot of affected board.
