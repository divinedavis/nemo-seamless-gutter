Gutter cleaning

A thorough cleaning keeps water flowing and gives us a chance to spot small problems before they grow. Here's what that includes:

  • Remove leaves and debris — clear out the buildup of leaves, needles, twigs, and grit that collects in the trough.
  • Flush the downspouts — run water through to break up and clear any clogs so the system drains freely.
  • Check the flow — confirm water moves toward the outlets and drains away from the foundation rather than pooling or overflowing.
  • Bag and haul off the debris — we clean up after ourselves and take the mess with us.

Gutter repair

If your gutters are still in good shape overall but a few things have gone wrong, repairs are often the practical fix. Common repairs we handle:

  • Re-secure sagging gutters — reattach and support runs that have started to droop.
  • Re-pitch for proper flow — adjust the slope so water runs to the downspouts instead of sitting in the gutter.
  • Reseal leaking seams and end caps — stop drips at joints and corners.
  • Replace damaged sections — swap out lengths that are dented, split, or rusted through.
  • Reattach or replace downspouts — fix loose, crushed, or missing downspouts so water gets carried away.
  • Fix gutters pulling away from the fascia — refasten gutters that have separated from the house.

Signs you need service

Not sure whether it's time? These are the things to watch for:

When to clean your gutters

Common questions

How do I know if the clog is in the gutter or the downspout?

If water spills over the gutter edge along its length, the trough is packed. If it backs up and overflows only near the corner, the downspout or the elbow at the bottom is plugged. Tapping the downspout tells you a lot — a dull thud means it's full.

My gutter is pulling away from the house. Does that mean replacement?

Usually not. Years of standing water and ice make a gutter heavy enough to loosen the fasteners, and the fix is often new hangers set into solid wood. If the fascia board behind it has gone soft, that board needs attention first or the new hangers won't hold.

What does clogged gutter repair cost?

It depends on how many feet of gutter, how many stories, roof pitch and access, how much debris is in there, and whether hangers, seams, end caps or fascia need work once it's clean. We look at it in person and put a free written estimate in your hands before anything starts.

How often should gutters be cleaned in York County?

Twice a year suits most homes here — late spring after the maples and seed pods drop, and again in late fall once the oaks finish. Houses tight under heavy tree cover often need a third pass. Homes with little overhang can sometimes stretch to once a year.

Will gutter guards mean I never have to clean them again?

No system is truly no-maintenance. Guards keep leaves and sticks out of the trough and cut down how often you're up there, but shingle grit, pollen and fine debris still collect, and the guards themselves need to be checked. Treat them as less cleaning, not zero.

Downspout Installation and Replacement in York, PA

Most of the water problems we get called about aren't caused by the gutter itself — they're caused by too few downspouts, undersized ones, or outlets that dump water right against the foundation. A gutter can only move what it can drain. If you have a long run on one side of the house with a single 2x3 downspout at the end, that trough is going to overflow in a hard rain no matter how clean it is. Adding a second outlet, or stepping up to a 3x4 downspout, usually fixes it for good. On homes with steep or large roof sections — common on older York and Hanover-area houses with additions — we'll size the outlets to the actual roof area feeding them rather than just matching what was there.

We install new downspouts on their own or as part of a gutter job, in matching colors so the run looks intentional instead of patched. That includes moving a downspout off a walkway or driveway, replacing crushed or dented sections, adding kickout flashing where a roof edge meets a wall, and tying the bottom into extensions, splash blocks, or an existing underground drain line. If water is currently discharging within a few feet of your foundation, that's the first thing we'll look at, because it's cheaper to redirect water than to deal with a wet basement later.

Cost depends on a handful of things we can walk through on site: how many stories the drop covers, whether the run needs elbows to get around bays, soffit returns, or porch roofs, whether you want standard 2x3 or oversized 3x4 aluminum, and whether the bottom ties into buried drainage or just discharges above grade. Estimates are free anywhere in York County — call Eric at (717) 578-0073 and we'll look at the roof line, not just the spot that's overflowing.

  • Upsizing 2x3 to 3x4 on large or steep roof sections

  • Extensions and buried drain tie-ins to move water away from the foundation

How many downspouts does my house actually need?

A common rule of thumb is one downspout for roughly every 30 to 40 feet of gutter, but roof area and pitch matter more than length alone. A steep roof or a large section draining into one gutter run moves water faster and needs more outlet capacity. When we're on site we look at what each run is catching and where the water has to go once it's down. In a lot of cases the fix is one added downspout, not a whole new gutter system.
